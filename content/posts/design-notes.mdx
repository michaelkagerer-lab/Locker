Building your own site is an exercise in constraint. You can do anything, so you have to decide what you actually want.

Here are some notes on how I thought about it.

## Start with Content, Not Aesthetics

The first mistake I made was opening a design tool before I'd written anything. I spent hours fiddling with color palettes and typeface combinations, producing something that looked nice but had no idea what it was for.

The second attempt started differently: I wrote three posts first, collected a small set of photos, and then asked myself what kind of container these things needed. The design followed from there, rather than the other way around.

This seems obvious in retrospect. Design is problem-solving, and you need to know the problem before you can solve it.

## Against Clutter

I have a strong reaction to sites that are trying to do too many things at once. The floating social share buttons. The newsletter pop-up that arrives before you've read anything. The sidebar with recent posts, tag clouds, author bios, related posts, and an advertisement for a course.

Each of these elements was probably added with good reason. But they accumulate into noise, and noise works against reading.

My goal was a site where the default state is *reading*. Everything else — navigation, tags, archive links — should exist but not compete.

## Typography First

If you're building a reading-oriented site, typography is the most important design decision you'll make. Everything else is decoration.

I spent a disproportionate amount of time on:

- **Line length.** Prose reads best at 60–75 characters per line. Wider than that, the eye has trouble tracking back to the start of the next line.
- **Line height.** Dense text is harder to read. I use a generous line height in the body copy — around 1.7.
- **Type scale.** Headings should feel meaningfully different from body text, not just slightly larger.

The specific typefaces matter less than getting these fundamentals right.

## Dark Mode

I waffled on whether to include a dark mode toggle. The argument against: it's a complexity you have to maintain, and most systems already have a preference.

The argument for: some people genuinely read better on light backgrounds; others on dark. Giving them the choice costs relatively little.

I landed on including the toggle, using the system preference as the default. It's a small thing, but it respects that readers are different.

## What I Cut

A partial list of things I built and removed:

- **Comments.** I don't want to moderate comments, and the best conversations happen elsewhere anyway. I'm reachable by email.
- **View counts.** Made me write for numbers instead of ideas.
- **A newsletter form.** RSS does the same thing without the inbox noise.
- **"Related posts" recommendations.** Felt algorithmic in a way that doesn't suit the site.

---

These notes are mostly for myself — a record of what I was thinking. If they're useful to anyone building something similar, so much the better.
